# ATHLETEN LEGAL DOCUMENT INDEX

This directory contains the principal legal and policy documents for AthleteN.

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `../LICENSE` | Proprietary software and intellectual-property licence |
| `TERMS_OF_SERVICE.md` | Service use, accounts, subscriptions, acceptable use, suspension, and contractual terms |
| `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` | Privacy, personal-data processing, children's data, rights, retention, and security disclosures |
| `CONTRIBUTOR_AGREEMENT.md` | Voluntary contribution, licensing, ownership, confidentiality, and security terms |
| `MEDICAL_AND_AI_DISCLAIMER.md` | Training, medical, safety, and AI-output limitations |
| `SECURITY.md` | Responsible vulnerability reporting and security-testing rules |

## Document hierarchy

These documents serve different purposes and should not be treated as interchangeable.

- The software licence governs intellectual-property rights in proprietary AthleteN material.
- The Terms of Service govern the relationship concerning use of the AthleteN service.
- The Privacy Policy describes personal-data processing and privacy practices.
- The Contributor Agreement governs qualifying voluntary contributions when accepted by both parties.
- The Medical and AI Disclaimer addresses the limits of informational and AI-assisted outputs.
- The Security Policy governs responsible security reporting and authorised testing.

Third-party licences, payment-provider terms, academy agreements, employment agreements, contractor agreements, and other specific written agreements may apply separately.

## Production launch checklist

Before public commercial launch, Nova Code should replace all `[INSERT ...]` placeholders, verify the final production data flows against the Privacy Policy, identify the correct legal entity and address, establish the required grievance/privacy/security contacts, confirm payment/refund disclosures, verify age and parental-consent flows, inventory third-party licences, and obtain qualified legal review of the final documents.

These documents must be kept consistent with the actual product. A policy must not promise a control, process, retention period, consent mechanism, security measure, or user right that the deployed product does not actually implement or provide.
